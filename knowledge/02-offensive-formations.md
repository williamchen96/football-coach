# Offensive formations

A formation is a personnel group plus a quarterback location plus a receiver distribution. The names vary by team; the structure does not.

## Quarterback location

| Set | Where | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Under center | Hands under the center | Downhill run game, the best play-action (the quarterback's back is turned and the back runs every track), quarterback sneaks |
| Shotgun | About 5 yards deep | Vision, quicker throws, read-option and RPO game. The back sits beside the quarterback, which tips the direction of some runs |
| Pistol | About 4 yards deep with the back directly behind | Shotgun vision with a downhill back who can go either way |

## Backfield sets (under center)

- **I formation**: fullback and tailback stacked behind the quarterback. **Strong I / Weak I (offset)**: fullback offset toward or away from the tight end.
- **Singleback (Ace)**: one back at about 7 yards.
- **Split backs (Pro)**: two backs side by side. A West Coast staple, rare now.
- **Full house / T / Wishbone**: three backs. Goal-line and option football.

In shotgun the back is described by side (strong or weak, or "near/far") and by depth. With two backs, one on each side of the quarterback is **split gun**.

## Receiver distributions

| Family | Distribution | Common names | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro | 2x1 with a tight end | Pro, Twins (both receivers same side) | Balanced run and pass from 21 or 12 |
| Doubles | 2x2 | Doubles, Deuce, Spread, Ace (two tight ends) | Balanced. The defense must show its shell honestly |
| Trips | 3x1, receivers only | Trips, Trio | Stresses zone rotation; isolates X on the back side |
| Trey | 3x1 with the tight end on the three-receiver side | Trey, Trips Closed | Run strength and pass strength on the same side |
| Bunch | Three receivers within a few yards of each other | Bunch, Cluster | Creates natural rubs against man, forces "banjo" switch rules |
| Stack | Two receivers one behind the other | Stack | Free release for the back receiver, confuses man assignments |
| Empty | 3x2, no back | Empty, Spread, Trey Empty | Forces the defense to declare, quick-game matchups. Five-man protection only |
| Quads | 4x1 | Quads | Overloads zone coverage to one side |
| Tight / condensed | Receivers pulled in near the line | Tight, Nasty, Reduced | Crack blocks, room to break outward, crossing routes, harder to press. Central to the Shanahan and McVay offenses |
| Wing | A tight end or back just outside and behind the end man | Wing, Wing Trips | An extra gap for the run game and a free release |
| Heavy / Jumbo | Extra tight ends or a sixth lineman | Heavy, Jumbo, Goal Line | Short yardage |
| Unbalanced | An ineligible lineman moved so the line is longer to one side | Tackle Over, Unbalanced | Forces the defense to adjust its front or be outnumbered |
| Wildcat | Direct snap to a runner | Wildcat | Gains a blocker by removing the non-running quarterback |

## Splits

- **Normal**: outside receivers near the bottom of the numbers; slots split the difference between the tackle and the outside receiver.
- **Plus split**: wider than normal, to open the middle or isolate a corner.
- **Minus (reduced) split**: tighter than normal, to run out-breaking routes with room or to block.
- **Nasty**: a receiver 2 to 3 yards outside the tackle or tight end.
- A receiver's split is a tell: wide splits favor in-breakers and fades, reduced splits favor out-breakers, crossers and crack blocks. Good offenses break their own tendencies.

## On and off the line

Seven players must be on the line and only the ends of the line are eligible. In a typical 11 personnel set, X is on the line on one side and the tight end is the end man on the other, so Z and the slot are off the line. Being off the line lets a receiver go in motion and gives him a cleaner release against press.

## Shifts and motion

A **shift** is two or more players resetting before the snap; all must then be set for a second. **Motion** is one player moving at the snap.

| Motion | What it is |
|---|---|
| Across (Zip, Zoom) | A receiver crosses the formation, changing strength |
| Short / Return (Yo-yo) | A receiver starts across and comes back |
| Jet (Fly) | A receiver runs flat and fast across the formation at the snap. Sets up jet sweep, and moves the second level before any run |
| Orbit | A receiver loops behind the quarterback or back, threatening a swing or reverse |
| Shuttle / Escort | A back or tight end motions out to the perimeter as a blocker or as a pass threat |
| Trade | The tight end moves from one side to the other, flipping run strength |
| Empty motion | The back motions out of the backfield, creating empty |

Why offenses move:

1. **Information**: a defender who follows across suggests man; a bump or rotation suggests zone.
2. **Leverage**: a receiver at a run arrives on a defender with better angles, and cannot be pressed.
3. **Changing the math**: motion can add a gap or a receiver to one side later than the defense can adjust.
4. **Eye discipline**: late movement pulls linebackers and safeties out of their fits, which is the engine of the wide zone and play-action offenses.
5. **Matchups**: moving a back or tight end wide shows who the defense puts on him.

## What formations ask of the defense

- A tight end adds a gap; two tight ends on opposite sides add two and leave the defense no obvious strength.
- Trips forces the defense to choose: rotate to the three-receiver side and leave the single receiver one on one, or stay balanced and be short to trips.
- Formation into the boundary puts the passing strength to the short side and tests whether the defense sets its coverage to the field or to the receivers.
- Empty removes run fits but takes the back out of protection, so the quarterback must own the sixth rusher.
- Condensed sets give receivers a two-way release and bring cornerbacks into the run fit, where many are uncomfortable.

## Tempo and cadence

- **Huddle**, **no huddle** (calling at the line), and **hurry-up** (two-minute tempo) control how much the defense can substitute and disguise.
- A **hard count** or **dummy cadence** draws the defense into showing a blitz, or offside for a free play.
- **Check with me**: the offense lines up with two plays and the quarterback picks at the line ("kill" switches to the second play, "can" is the same idea in other systems).
