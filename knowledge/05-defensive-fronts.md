# Defensive fronts

The front is the alignment of the linemen and linebackers. Its job against the run is to put one defender in every gap; against the pass it is the rush plan. Read `01-fundamentals.md` for gap letters and technique numbers.

## One-gap and two-gap

- **One-gap**: each lineman attacks a single gap. Linebackers fit the gaps that are left. Produces penetration and tackles for loss; a missed fit leaves a hole.
- **Two-gap**: a lineman plays head-up, controls the blocker and is responsible for the gap on either side, shedding to the ball. It frees linebackers and lets a defense play the run with fewer bodies. It needs big, strong linemen and gives up pass rush.

Most modern defenses mix them: one-gap on the play side, a "gap and a half" for others.

## Even fronts (four down linemen)

| Front | Alignment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Over | 3-technique and Sam to the tight-end side; shade nose (1-technique) to the weak side; ends in 5 and 6 or 9 | The basic 4-3 look. Strong against strong-side runs; the weak-side B gap is the bubble |
| Under | 3-technique to the weak side; nose shaded to the strong side; Sam on the line over the tight end; weak end wide | Five men on the line. The base of the Seattle and Pete Carroll defenses, with the weak end ("Leo") as the featured rusher |
| Even (G) | Both tackles in 2 or 2i techniques | Protects the linebackers from guards; balanced |
| Wide 9 | Ends aligned far outside the tackle or tight end | Maximum pass-rush width; creates big B and C gaps that linebackers must fill |

## Odd fronts (three down linemen)

| Front | Alignment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Okie (base 3-4) | Nose in 0, ends in 4 or 5, two outside linebackers on the edges | The classic two-gap 3-4 |
| One-gap 3-4 | The same people shifted into Over or Under spacing | The Wade Phillips approach: 3-4 personnel, 4-3 rules |
| Tite | Nose in 0, ends in 4i | Closes both B gaps, forcing runs to bounce outside to free-running linebackers. A common answer to spread zone runs |
| Mint | Tite spacing from nickel personnel | Popularized by Nick Saban; the standard modern college and pro odd nickel |
| Bear (46) | Linemen cover the center and both guards (3-0-3) with edges outside | Eliminates interior double teams and pullers. Exposes the defense to perimeter runs and play-action behind single coverage |
| 5-1 (Penny) | Five on the line (three linemen and two stand-up edges) with one off-ball linebacker | Associated with the Fangio and Staley family. Plays the run with a light box while keeping two deep safeties |
| 6-1 | Six on the line | A game-plan answer to wide zone teams: every lineman is covered, so there are no free combination blocks |

## Sub-package fronts

- **Nickel 4-2-5**: four linemen, two linebackers. The standard defense against 11 personnel.
- **Nickel 2-4-5 or 3-3-5**: odd spacing with stand-up edges; the fourth rusher can come from anywhere.
- **Dime 4-1-6 and 3-2-6**: long-yardage rush fronts, often with linemen moved to create one-on-ones.
- **NASCAR** and similar: four pass rushers on the line with ends playing inside, on passing downs.
- **Goal line 6-2 or 5-3**: every gap filled, corners in man.

## Run fits

A fit is the gap each of the front players and support defenders owns after the blocks declare.

- **Force (contain)**: the defender responsible for turning the ball back inside. Named by who has it: **Sky** (safety), **Cloud** (corner), **Buzz** or backer force.
- **Alley**: the defender who fills between the force player and the interior, usually a safety.
- **Cutback (fold)**: the back-side defender who takes away the cutback, often the back-side safety or linebacker.
- **Spill**: defenders attack blockers with their outside shoulder, forcing the ball to bounce wide to unblocked pursuit. Used by fast, aggressive fronts and against pullers.
- **Box**: the edge defender takes on blockers with his inside shoulder and keeps the ball inside him. The traditional answer when help is inside.
- **Squeeze and scrape (gap exchange)**: against zone read, the end crashes on the back while a linebacker scrapes outside for the quarterback. It makes the quarterback's read wrong.
- **Fitting from two-high**: quarters safeties read run and become the eighth and ninth fitters late. This is how a defense shows a light box and still gets numbers to the run, and it is what play-action posts attack.
- **Plus-one**: a defense wants one more fitter than the offense has blockers. With a single deep safety it has it; with two deep it must get it late, by stunting, or by winning blocks.

Against pullers, the rule is to add a fitter where the puller arrives: linebackers "fast flow" over the top or "fill and spill" underneath, by game plan.

## Line movement and stunts

| Term | Description |
|---|---|
| Slant / angle | The whole line steps one gap in one direction |
| Pinch | Linemen step inside |
| Twist (game) | Two rushers exchange lanes; one penetrates to pick a blocker, the other loops behind |
| T-E (TEX) | The tackle goes first, the end loops inside |
| E-T (EX) | The end crashes first, the tackle loops outside |
| T-T | The two tackles twist |
| Pirate | Both ends and tackles slant inside with linebackers looping out |
| Stem | Linemen shift alignment just before the snap to change the blocking rules |
| Long stick | An end slants across two gaps, often into the A or B gap as part of a pressure |

Stunts change the gaps after the snap. Zone blocking handles them better than man or gap schemes, and slide protection handles them better than man protection.

## The edge

- A **five-technique** end sets a hard edge and squeezes down blocks.
- A **wide** rusher gets up the field; the offense attacks the space inside him with draws, traps and inside runs.
- Against bootlegs, the back-side edge has **contain**: he does not chase the run fake flat down the line unless a teammate replaces him.

## How offenses attack a front

- **Bubbles**: an uncovered gap with a linebacker behind it. Run combination blocks at it.
- **Shade and 3-technique**: run zone to the shade for an easy double team, or gap schemes at the 3-technique for a down-block angle.
- **Wide ends**: trap, draw, inside zone.
- **Bear and loaded boxes**: throw. One-on-one coverage outside, and perimeter runs with crack blocks.
- **Light boxes with two deep**: run until a safety comes down, then throw behind him.
