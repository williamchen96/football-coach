# The run game

Every run is one of two ideas. **Zone** schemes block areas and let the back choose the hole. **Gap** schemes block down to create a wall and bring a puller to a chosen hole. A complete run game has some of each, because each punishes the way defenses play the other.

## Blocking vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base (drive) | Block the man over you, straight ahead |
| Down | Block the first defender to your inside, sealing him away from the hole |
| Reach | Get your helmet outside a defender aligned to your play side and hook him |
| Cutoff / scoop | Back-side block that takes away pursuit by crossing the defender's face |
| Combo | Two linemen start on one defensive lineman and one comes off to a linebacker |
| Double team | Two linemen stay on one defender and drive him back, sometimes into a linebacker's path |
| Ace, Deuce, Trey | Combos by pair: center and guard, guard and tackle, tackle and tight end |
| Climb | Leave the first level for a linebacker |
| Pull | Leave your spot and run behind the line to block somewhere else |
| Kick-out | A puller or fullback blocks the end man from the inside out, opening a lane inside him |
| Log | A puller hooks the end man from the outside when that defender squeezes down, taking the run outside |
| Wrap (lead through) | A puller turns up inside the kick-out and blocks the play-side linebacker |
| Skip pull | Pulling with a crossover step to stay square, used by backside pullers on power |
| Hinge | Back-side tackle steps down to protect the gap, then pivots back to wall off the end |
| Fold | A lineman steps around an adjacent teammate's down block to reach a linebacker |
| Crack | A receiver blocks down on a linebacker or safety from outside |
| Stalk | A receiver's sustained block on a defensive back |
| Arc | A tight end or back releases outside the end man to block the force defender |
| Insert | A tight end or fullback leads through an interior gap onto a linebacker |
| Sift / slice | A tight end or H-back comes across the formation behind the line to block the back-side end |
| Wham | A tight end or H-back traps an interior lineman who was left unblocked |

## Zone schemes

### Inside zone

- **Blocking**: every lineman steps play side. A lineman who is covered blocks that defender; one who is uncovered helps on the adjacent defensive lineman and climbs to a linebacker. The result is two combos working vertically to the play-side and back-side linebackers.
- **Back**: aims at the inside leg of the play-side guard (the play-side A gap), pressing the line, and reads the first down lineman past the center. His choices are **bang** (hit the designed crease), **bend** (cut back behind the center), or **bounce** (outside), in that order of likelihood.
- **Why it works**: vertical movement at the point of attack and a cutback lane when linebackers over-flow.
- **Variants**:
  - **Split zone**: a tight end or H-back sifts across to kick out the back-side end, sealing the cutback.
  - **Zone read**: the back-side end is left unblocked and read by the quarterback. End crashes: quarterback keeps. End stays: give.
  - **Lock**: the back-side tackle man-blocks the end, used when there is no read or sift.
  - **Insert / iso zone**: a fullback or H-back leads on a linebacker.

### Outside zone (wide zone, stretch)

- **Blocking**: every lineman takes a wide lateral step and tries to reach the defender to his play side. If he cannot reach him, he runs him to the sideline. Back-side linemen cut off.
- **Back**: aims at the outside leg of the tight end (or a yard outside the tackle), reads the end man on the line and then the next defender inside. Rule: one cut and go. Bounce if the edge is reached, bang if the edge is set, bend if the defense over-runs.
- **Why it works**: it stretches every gap sideways until one opens, and it makes every play look the same for the first two seconds. It is the foundation of the Shanahan family of offenses because bootlegs, play-action crossers and screens all start from the identical look.
- **Variants**: **mid zone** (aiming point at the tackle, a tighter track), **toss** (pitch instead of handoff, getting the back outside faster), **pin and pull** (when a lineman has a defender inside him he blocks down and the uncovered linemen pull around).

### Duo

Looks like inside zone but is a gap scheme: two true double teams on the interior defensive linemen driving them into the linebackers, the tight end on the end, no puller. The back presses the line and reads the Mike: if the Mike fills inside, bounce outside the double team; if the Mike scrapes over the top, hit it downhill. One perimeter defender, usually a corner, is unblocked and belongs to the back. Often described as "power without a puller".

## Gap schemes

### Power

- **Blocking**: the play side blocks down, with a double team on the play-side defensive tackle that works to the back-side linebacker. A fullback or H-back kicks out the end man on the line. The back-side guard skip-pulls and leads through the hole onto the play-side linebacker. The center blocks back for the pulling guard. The back-side tackle hinges.
- **Back**: takes a step to let the puller clear, then follows the guard inside the kick-out.
- **Why it works**: it adds two blockers at the point of attack and creates angles on every block.
- **One-back power**: with no fullback, the tight end blocks the end, or the end is read (power read).

### Counter

- **GT counter**: the back-side guard pulls and kicks out the end man; the back-side tackle pulls and wraps to the play-side linebacker. The play side blocks down. A tight end or H-back fills for the pulling tackle, or the quarterback reads the back-side end.
- **GH counter**: the guard kicks out and an H-back or tight end wraps. This keeps the back-side tackle home to protect against a chasing end.
- **Back**: sells a step away, then follows the pullers. The misdirection step holds the linebackers long enough for the down blocks to seal them.

### Other gap runs

| Play | Idea |
|---|---|
| Trap | An interior defensive lineman is left unblocked, then kicked out by a pulling guard. Punishes penetrating three-techniques |
| Wham | Same idea with a tight end or H-back as the trapper |
| Iso (lead) | Linemen base block, leaving one linebacker for the fullback. The back follows the fullback |
| Dart | Tackle pull version of power from one-back sets |
| Pin and pull | Down blocks by covered linemen, pulls by uncovered ones, to the perimeter |
| Buck sweep | Both guards pull, the first kicks out or logs, the second leads |
| Crack toss | Receivers crack down on the edge, a tackle or guard pulls for the corner, quick pitch to the back |
| Draw | Linemen show pass sets, the back delays, then runs into a defense that is rushing up the field and dropping |
| Sneak | Quarterback follows the center's wedge; short yardage. The push-assisted version has remained legal through 2025 |

## Quarterback run game and option

Using the quarterback as a runner gains a blocker, because the defense must account for him. The unblocked defender is "blocked" by being read.

| Play | Read defender | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Zone read | Back-side end | End crashes: keep. End stays: give |
| Read with a bubble or arc | Back-side end, then a flat defender | Adds a third option, making it a triple option |
| Power read (inverted veer) | Play-side end | Back runs the sweep path, quarterback runs power inside. End widens: quarterback keeps inside |
| Counter read / bash | Back-side end | Back goes away from the pullers; quarterback follows the pullers if the end chases the back |
| Speed option | Force defender | Quarterback attacks his outside shoulder and pitches if he commits |
| Triple option (veer, midline) | Dive key, then pitch key | Give, keep or pitch |
| Quarterback draw, quarterback power, quarterback counter | None | Designed runs with the back as a lead blocker, common in the red zone |

## Counting the box

Before the snap the offense asks three questions:

1. **Numbers**: do we have a blocker for every box defender? With a non-running quarterback, the defense has one more than the offense can block whenever it plays one deep safety. The extra defender must be read, held by a pass threat, or run away from.
2. **Angles**: where are the defensive linemen aligned? Run zone toward a 3-technique's bubble or toward the shade depending on the combination you want; run gap schemes where the down blocks have angles.
3. **Grass**: where is the open space? Two deep safeties and a light box say run. A loaded box with one-on-one coverage outside says throw.

## RPOs (run-pass options)

The line blocks a run while the quarterback reads one defender who cannot be right.

- **Pre-snap RPO (a gift)**: a quick screen or hitch attached to a run. If the defense does not cover the receiver down, throw it now.
- **Post-snap RPO**: the quarterback rides the mesh and reads a "conflict" defender, usually a weak hook linebacker or a down safety. Defender fits the run: pull and throw into the space he left (glance, slant, stick, pop). Defender stays in the throwing lane: hand off.
- Common tags: **bubble**, **now screen**, **hitch**, **slant**, **glance** (a five-step skinny post behind the linebackers), **stick**, **pop** to the tight end.
- The NFL allows linemen only 1 yard downfield, so pro RPOs must be thrown quickly.
- Defenses answer by playing man coverage, by having the conflict defender slow-play, or by changing who the conflict defender is after the snap.

## Constraint plays off the run

Each base run should have a play that punishes the defense for stopping it:

- Linebackers flow fast to outside zone: bootleg, play-action crosser, cutback, screen back the other way.
- End crashes on zone: read him, or sift a tight end onto him.
- Safeties fit the run aggressively: play-action post, glance RPO.
- Corners sit off: bubble and now screens.
- Edge defenders chase pullers: reverse, counter, naked bootleg.
