# The pass game

A pass play is three things working on one clock: routes that stress a defender or a coverage, a protection that buys the time those routes need, and a quarterback's drop and read that match both.

## The route tree

The common numbered tree. Odd numbers break outside, even numbers break inside. Systems differ in the details, so treat the numbers as one widely used convention.

| # | Route | Depth | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Hitch | 5-6 | Run off the corner, stop, turn back to the quarterback |
| 1 | Flat (quick out) | 3-5 | Short break to the sideline |
| 2 | Slant | 3 steps | Break inside at 45 degrees |
| 3 | Comeback | 15-18, back to 13-15 | Sell the go, break back toward the sideline |
| 4 | Curl | 12-14 | Stop and work back inside to the quarterback |
| 5 | Out | 10-12 | Square break to the sideline |
| 6 | Dig (in) | 12-15 | Square break across the middle |
| 7 | Corner (flag) | Break at 10-12 | Angle to the back pylon |
| 8 | Post | Break at 10-12 | Angle to the goalpost |
| 9 | Go (fly, streak) | Deep | Straight down the field |

## Named routes

| Route | Description |
|---|---|
| Fade | Outside-release vertical thrown to the outside shoulder. **Back-shoulder fade**: thrown short and outside on purpose when the corner is on top |
| Seam | Vertical by an inside receiver, up the hash |
| Bender | A seam that bends across the face of a safety when the middle is open |
| Skinny post (glance) | A post with a shallow angle, thrown on rhythm behind the linebackers |
| Drag / shallow | A crossing route at 2 to 5 yards |
| Over / deep cross | A crossing route that climbs to 15 to 20 yards |
| Drive | A shallow cross with a dig behind it from the same side |
| Stick | Inside receiver pushes to 5 or 6 yards and turns out, or sits against zone |
| Spot (snag) | A slant stem that settles at 5 to 6 yards inside |
| Whip (pivot) | Start inside like a slant, plant and break back out |
| Return (jerk) | Start outside or across, stop, return the other way |
| Option (choice) | The receiver breaks in, out or sits depending on the defender's leverage |
| Texas (angle) | A back releases toward the flat, then breaks sharply back inside |
| Wheel | A flat route that turns up the sideline |
| Swing | A back bows out wide behind the line |
| Arrow | A flat route run at a shallow angle from the snap |
| Bubble | A receiver bows backward and outside for a quick screen |
| Sail | A deep out or corner at 12 to 15 yards, the middle level of a flood |
| Sluggo | Slant and go |
| Stutter-go, out and up, hitch and go | Double moves off the short route |
| Post-corner | Sell the post, break to the corner. **Corner-post (dino)** is the reverse |
| Check-down | A back or tight end settles underneath after checking protection |

## Drops and timing

The quarterback's footwork is the clock. The ball should come out on the last step or one hitch after it.

| Drop | From shotgun | Routes it times with |
|---|---|---|
| Three-step (quick game) | Catch and one step | Hitch, slant, quick out, fade, stick |
| Five-step | Three steps | Curl, dig, out, comeback, corner |
| Seven-step | Five steps | Deep posts, double moves, deep crossers |
| Play-action | Fake, then set | Shot plays and intermediate crossers |
| Sprint-out / bootleg | Moving launch point | Flood concepts; cuts the field in half and changes the rush angles |

## Concepts

Pass concepts combine routes to put one defender, or one coverage, in a bind. Most are one of four ideas.

### Quick game (three-step)

| Concept | Routes | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Slant-flat | Outside slant, inside flat | Flat defender: he widens with the flat, throw the slant; he sits in the slant window, throw the flat |
| Double slants | Two slants on one side | Inside slant first, outside slant second as the linebacker reacts |
| Hitch | Hitches outside | Thrown against off coverage |
| Stick | Outside go, #2 flat, #3 stick | Flat defender: widens, throw stick; hangs inside, throw flat |
| Spacing | Five short routes spread evenly across the field | Find the open grass between zone droppers |
| Snag (spot) | Corner, spot, flat forming a triangle | Corner if the cornerback squats; otherwise spot against flat |
| Quick out / fade-out | Fade outside, quick out inside | Leverage read on the slot defender |

### Horizontal stretches (make one underneath defender cover two)

| Concept | Routes | Best against |
|---|---|---|
| Curl-flat | Outside curl, inside flat | Cover 3 and Cover 4 flat defenders |
| All curls / Hank | Curls outside, hook in the middle, flats | Soft zone |
| Mesh | Two crossers rubbing at 5 to 6 yards, a corner or sit route over the top, a back to the flat or wheel | Man coverage (the rub); zone when the crossers settle |
| Shallow cross | A shallow from one side, a dig from the other | Man, and zone hook defenders |

### Vertical stretches (high-low one defender, or flood a zone)

| Concept | Routes | Best against |
|---|---|---|
| Smash | Outside hitch, inside corner | Cover 2: high-low the squat corner |
| Flood (sail) | Go, sail, flat to one side | Cover 3: three receivers through two zones |
| Levels | In-breaking routes at two depths | Zone hook defenders |
| Drive | Shallow and dig from the same side | Hook defender high-low |
| Dagger | Inside seam clears, outside receiver runs a deep dig behind it, a shallow underneath | Cover 3, Cover 1, Cover 4 with the safety carried |
| Y-Cross | Tight end deep cross, back-side post or go, flat control | Man and single-high zone |
| Mills (PIN: post and in) | Inside dig to pull down a safety, outside post behind him | Quarters |
| Four verticals | Four receivers on landmarks | Cover 3 (two seams against one safety), Cover 2 with a bender |
| Scissors | Outside post and inside corner crossing | Quarters and man switches |
| Yankee | Deep post with a deep cross under it, usually off play-action with max protection | Single-high: the post safety cannot cover both |
| Post-wheel | Outside post clears, inside receiver or back wheels | Cover 3 corners who carry the post |
| Switch verticals | Two receivers cross releases and run verticals | Pattern-match rules |

### Man beaters

Crossers and meshes, rubs from bunch and stack, slants against inside-shaded press help, option routes for slots and backs, back-shoulder fades, wheels and Texas routes for backs on linebackers, and motion that makes a defender run through traffic.

## How the quarterback reads

There are three ways to organize a read, and most plays combine them:

1. **Pre-snap side selection**: the play has a different concept on each side. The safeties (MOFO or MOFC) and the corners' leverage tell the quarterback which side to work.
2. **Key-defender read**: the concept high-lows or stretches one defender. The quarterback watches him and throws where he is not.
3. **Pure progression**: 1 to 2 to 3 to check-down, tied to footwork, regardless of coverage.

**Alerts** are routes thrown only if the pre-snap look gives them away, such as a go route against press with no safety over the top.

**Hot routes and sight adjusts**: when the defense brings more rushers than the protection can block, a designated receiver breaks his route off short into the space the blitzer left, and the quarterback throws it immediately. Both must see the same thing.

## Protection

Protection is named by how many block and how the line sorts its assignments. The center or quarterback identifies the Mike to set the count.

| Protection | Blockers | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Full slide | 5 or 6 | Every lineman blocks the gap to one side. The back or tight end takes the edge away from the slide |
| Half slide | 6 | One side of the line plays man on the defensive linemen across from them ("big on big"); the center and the other side slide to their gaps. The back reads the linebackers on the man side, inside to outside |
| Big on big (BOB) | 6 | Linemen take down linemen, backs take linebackers |
| Five-man (scat, empty) | 5 | All five eligibles release. The quarterback owns any sixth rusher with a hot throw |
| Seven-man (max) | 7 | A back and a tight end stay in, often with a check-release. Used for shot plays |
| Play-action | 6 to 8 | The line shows run blocking; aggressive on the first level, with the fake holding the second |
| Sprint-out | 6 or 7 | The line reaches to the launch side; the back seals the edge |

Rules of thumb:

- The offense can protect only as many as it keeps in. If the defense rushes one more, the ball must come out hot, or the protection must be redirected ("slide to the pressure").
- A back who has no rusher to block **check-releases** into a route.
- **Chips** by a back or tight end on an edge rusher buy time without committing a full blocker.
- Twists and stunts are passed off by linemen staying on the same level and communicating; they beat man-blocked sides more easily than slide sides.

## Screens

Screens punish a hard rush and aggressive linebackers. Linemen sell pass protection, release, and lead downfield.

| Screen | Description |
|---|---|
| Slip (running back) screen | The back sets as a blocker, slips behind the rush, three linemen lead |
| Tunnel (jailbreak) | An outside receiver comes back inside behind releasing linemen |
| Bubble / now | Immediate throw to a receiver on the perimeter with receivers blocking |
| Tight end screen | Tight end blocks, releases behind the linebackers who dropped |
| Throwback / double screen | Action one way, screen back the other |

## Play-action

A run fake that holds or moves linebackers and safeties, opening the space behind them. It works off the look of the run more than off the run's statistics: defenders react to blocking and backfield action.

- **Drop-back play-action**: fake, set, throw digs, crossers and posts into the vacated hook zones.
- **Bootleg (keeper)**: fake one way, quarterback rolls the other with a flood: a flat route, a crossing route at 12 to 15 yards, and a deep route. **Naked** means no blocker for the back-side end.
- **Shot plays**: maximum protection with two or three deep routes (Yankee, post-cross, leak, where a tight end slips out the back side late).
- The best play-action looks exactly like the offense's best run for as long as possible: same formation, same motion, same line steps.
