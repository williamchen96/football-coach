# Fundamentals

The vocabulary everything else is built on.

## The field

- 100 yards goal line to goal line, 10-yard end zones, 53 1/3 yards wide.
- NFL hash marks are 18 feet 6 inches apart, in line with the goalposts. The ball is spotted on or between the hashes. Because NFL hashes are narrow, the "wide side" advantage is much smaller than in college.
- **Field** is the wide side when the ball is on a hash; **boundary** is the short side.
- The yard-line numbers are 2 yards tall, with their bottoms 12 yards from each sideline. Receivers and defensive backs use the numbers, the hashes and the sideline as landmarks for splits and for zone drops.
- **Red zone**: opponent's 20 to the goal line. **Backed up**: inside your own 10. **Plus territory**: the opponent's side of the 50.

## Offensive positions

Eleven players. Five offensive linemen are ineligible to catch a pass: left tackle (LT), left guard (LG), center (C), right guard (RG), right tackle (RT). The other six are the quarterback and five eligible receivers, labelled by letter:

| Letter | Role |
|---|---|
| QB | Quarterback |
| X | Split end. On the line of scrimmage, usually alone on the weak side. Often the best receiver against press |
| Z | Flanker. Off the line, usually to the strong side, free to go in motion |
| Y | Tight end, usually attached to the line next to a tackle |
| H (or F) | The flexible fourth piece: slot receiver in three-receiver sets, fullback or move tight end in heavier sets |
| RB (or HB, T) | Running back |
| FB | Fullback, a lead blocker aligned in front of the running back |
| U | Second tight end |

## Personnel groupings

Offensive personnel is named by two digits: number of running backs, then number of tight ends. Wide receivers are whatever is left of five.

| Name | RB | TE | WR | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 1 | 1 | 3 | The league's base offense. Forces the defense into nickel |
| 12 | 1 | 2 | 2 | Run and play-action balance; makes the defense choose base or nickel |
| 21 | 2 | 1 | 2 | Fullback offense: lead runs, play-action |
| 13 | 1 | 3 | 1 | Heavy run, shot plays off play-action |
| 22 | 2 | 2 | 1 | Short yardage, goal line |
| 10 | 1 | 0 | 4 | Spread passing |
| 20 | 2 | 0 | 3 | Two-back spread |
| 00 or 01 | 0 | 0/1 | 5/4 | Empty backfield |

Adding a sixth offensive lineman ("jumbo", "heavy") is reported to the referee, and that player is ineligible unless he reports as eligible.

Defensive personnel is named by the number of defensive backs:

- **Base**: 4 DBs with seven front players (4-3 or 3-4).
- **Nickel**: 5 DBs. The fifth is the nickel or slot corner ("Star"). This is the true base defense of the modern NFL, played on well over half of snaps.
- **Big nickel**: the fifth DB is a third safety rather than a corner.
- **Dime**: 6 DBs. **Quarter**: 7 DBs.

The matchup of offensive personnel against defensive personnel is the first decision of every play: heavy offense against small defense favors the run, light offense against big defense favors the pass.

## Defensive positions

- **Interior line**: nose tackle (NT), defensive tackle (DT). **Edge**: defensive end (DE) in four-man fronts, outside linebacker in 3-4 fronts.
- **Linebackers**: Mike (middle), Sam (strong side), Will (weak side). In 3-4 teams also Jack or Buck for the rush linebacker.
- **Secondary**: cornerbacks (CB), free safety (FS), strong safety (SS), nickel (N or Star), dime.

## Gaps

Gaps are the spaces between offensive linemen, lettered outward from the center on both sides:

- **A gap**: center to guard
- **B gap**: guard to tackle
- **C gap**: tackle to tight end (or outside the tackle if there is no tight end)
- **D gap**: outside the tight end

Every run defense assigns one defender to every gap. An offense creates an extra gap each time it adds a blocker (a tight end, a fullback, a pulling guard), and the defense must answer with an extra fitter.

## Techniques (defensive line alignment)

A number describes where a defender lines up relative to an offensive lineman:

| Technique | Alignment |
|---|---|
| 0 | Head-up on the center |
| 1 (shade) | Shoulder of the center |
| 2i | Inside shoulder of the guard |
| 2 | Head-up on the guard |
| 3 | Outside shoulder of the guard |
| 4i | Inside shoulder of the tackle |
| 4 | Head-up on the tackle |
| 5 | Outside shoulder of the tackle |
| 6 | Head-up on the tight end |
| 7 | Inside shoulder of the tight end |
| 9 | Outside shoulder of the tight end, or wide of the tackle |

Linebacker depth alignments add a zero: a 30 technique is a linebacker stacked over the outside shoulder of the guard.

## Strength

- **Run strength**: the side with the tight end, or more blockers.
- **Pass strength**: the side with more eligible receivers.
- **Field strength**: the wide side.

Defenses declare a strength before every snap ("Liz/Rip", "Lucky/Ringo") to set the front and the coverage. Offenses change the strength with shifts and motion to force late communication.

## Counting receivers and describing sets

- Receivers are numbered from the sideline in: #1, #2, #3.
- Distribution is written strong side by weak side: **2x2** (doubles), **3x1** (trips), **3x2** (empty), **4x1** (quads), **2x1** and **1x1** in two-back sets.
- A back who releases becomes the last number on his side, which is how a 2x2 set becomes 3x2 after the snap.

## Reading the defense before the snap

1. **Personnel**: who is on the field.
2. **Shell**: one deep safety (MOFC: likely Cover 1 or Cover 3) or two (MOFO: likely Cover 2, Cover 4, Cover 6, or 2-Man).
3. **Box count**: defenders within about a yard outside the tight ends and about 5 yards deep. Offense minus quarterback has at most the same number of blockers as the box in a neutral look; an extra defender means a run needs a read or a throw.
4. **Leverage and depth of corners**: press or off, inside or outside.
5. **Pressure indicators**: walked-up linebackers, a safety creeping, a nickel cheating in, defenders with eyes on the quarterback.

Motion gives one more clue: a defender who runs across the formation with the motion man suggests man coverage; a bump or a rotation suggests zone.

## Rules that shape scheme

- **Seven on the line**: the offense needs at least seven players on the line of scrimmage. Only the two players at the ends of the line and the players in the backfield are eligible. A tight end with a receiver on the line outside him is "covered" and ineligible.
- **Eligible numbers**: players wearing 50 to 79 are ineligible unless they report.
- **Motion and shifts**: one player may be moving at the snap, and not toward the line. After a shift by two or more players, everyone must be set for a full second.
- **Play clock**: 40 seconds from the end of the previous play, 25 after an administrative stoppage.
- **Illegal contact**: a defender may not contact a receiver more than 5 yards downfield while the quarterback is in the pocket. This is why NFL press technique is won or lost at the line.
- **Defensive pass interference** is a spot foul. **Defensive holding** is 5 yards and an automatic first down.
- **Ineligible downfield**: linemen may be no more than 1 yard downfield on a forward pass thrown beyond the line. This limits how long an NFL RPO can develop.
- **Intentional grounding**: a quarterback under pressure must throw toward an eligible receiver unless he is outside the tackle box and the ball reaches the line of scrimmage.
- **Blocking**: no blocks in the back, no chop blocks, no blocks below the waist outside tightly defined areas, no crackback blocks that are low or to the head.
