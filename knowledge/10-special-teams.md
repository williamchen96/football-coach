# Special teams

Roughly one play in six. Special teams decide field position, and field position decides how many points a drive is worth. Kicking rules have changed more than any others in recent seasons, so confirm the details for the current year.

## Kickoff

The NFL adopted the "dynamic kickoff" in 2024:

- The kicker kicks from his own 35. The other ten coverage players line up on the receiving team's 40 and may not move until the ball is touched or hits the ground in the landing zone.
- The receiving team sets up between its 35 and 30, with up to two returners deep.
- The **landing zone** runs from the goal line to the 20. A kick that lands there must be returned. A kick that falls short of it is treated like a kick out of bounds and comes out to the 40.
- A kick into the end zone that is downed is a touchback. The touchback spot was moved from the 30 to the 35 in 2025 to encourage returnable kicks.

What this changed: speed off the line matters less and blocking angles matter more, so returns look like run plays with double teams and a returner reading a crease. Kickers aim for the landing zone corner and vary hang time and bounce.

**Onside kick**: under the current format the kicking team must declare it, and it uses a traditional alignment. Recovery rates are low, around one in ten or less when the receiving team expects it.

**Safety kick**: after a safety the scored-upon team kicks from its own 20, by punt or placekick.

## Punt

- **Formation**: NFL teams use a spread punt with two **gunners** split wide, a personal protector behind the line, and the punter at about 15 yards.
- **Operation time**: snap to kick in about 2.0 seconds or less. Longer invites a block.
- **Coverage**: gunners beat single or double ("vise") jams to the returner; interior players cover in lanes; someone has contain on each side.
- **Punter's tools**: hang time over distance, directional punts to the sideline, and pooch or "coffin corner" kicks to down the ball inside the 10. A net of 40 or more yards is good.
- **Return side**: choose between a return (hold up the gunners, set a wall or a middle crease) and a block (overload a gap or come off the edge). A returner fair-catches when coverage is on him and lets the ball go when standing at or inside his own 10.
- **Fakes**: a direct snap to the protector, or a punter's pass, against a return unit that turns and runs early.

## Field goal and extra point

- The extra point is snapped from the 15, a 33-yard kick. A two-point try is from the 2.
- Field-goal distance is the line of scrimmage plus about 17 or 18 yards: 10 for the end zone and 7 or 8 for the snap.
- Operation time is about 1.3 seconds from snap to kick.
- The rush comes off the edge or tries to push the interior for a low kick. Leaping over the line is restricted.
- A missed field goal gives the opponent the ball at the spot of the kick, which makes long attempts costly.
- **Fakes**: the holder runs or throws, or a direct snap to the kicker or an upback.
- Kicker range has grown: attempts from beyond 55 yards are now routine in good conditions, which moves the edge of "four-down territory" back.

## Other plays

- **Fair-catch kick**: after a fair catch a team may attempt an uncontested field goal from that spot. Rare, but worth knowing at the end of a half.
- **Squib and sky kicks**: used late in halves to take time off the clock and keep the ball from a dangerous returner.
- **Hands team**: the receiving unit for an expected onside kick.

## Hidden yardage

The yards gained and lost on kicks, returns and penalties do not show in offensive statistics but move the starting field position of every drive.

- A drive starting at a team's own 20 scores far less often than one starting at its 40.
- A 5-yard edge in average starting field position over a game is worth about as much as a turnover.
- Penalties on returns are the most common way to lose it.

## What a coach wants from the units

- No blocked kicks, no penalties, no returns allowed for a touchdown.
- The core players are backup linebackers, safeties, tight ends and receivers. Their speed and tackling are why roster spots 46 through 53 are chosen partly for special teams.
- Situational awareness: where to down a punt, when to stay away from a bouncing ball, when to take a touchback.
